# Northwind Bank — Credit Improvement Guide (Sample)

> Fictional sample guidance for demonstrating retrieval-augmented advising.

## Lowering Credit Utilization

Utilization — how much of your available credit limit you are using — is one of
the fastest factors to improve. Bringing utilization **below 30%** moves most
applicants toward Near-Prime eligibility, and **below 15%** supports Prime.
Practical steps:

- Pay balances down before the statement closing date, not just the due date.
- Request a credit-limit increase on existing cards (without raising spending).
- Avoid closing old cards, which reduces total available credit.

## Resolving Late Payments

Payment history is the single heaviest driver of default risk at Northwind.

- Any **90+ day** delinquency is a hard barrier to Near-Prime and above; bring
  these current as the top priority.
- Set up autopay for at least the minimum to prevent new 30–59 day lates.
- Most negative marks age out of scoring impact over 24 months of clean history,
  so consistency matters more than a single large payment.

## Reducing Debt-to-Income

Debt-to-income (DTI) is the share of income committed to debt payments. Target
**0.40 or below** for Near-Prime and **0.30 or below** for Prime.

- Pay down the highest-payment revolving accounts first.
- Avoid taking on new installment loans before applying.
- Increasing documented income (raise, second income) lowers DTI directly.

## What Has Limited or No Effect

- **Age and dependents** are not actionable and should not be presented as
  things the applicant can change. Focus advice on utilization, late payments,
  and DTI.
- Opening several new accounts at once can temporarily *raise* risk; space out
  new credit applications.

## Realistic Timelines

- Utilization changes can reflect within one or two statement cycles.
- Recovering from a recent 90+ day late typically takes 12–24 months of clean
  payments. Set expectations accordingly when advising applicants.
